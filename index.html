A few things the system shows

In a national test of 199 drinks, 18 had more than 5 parts per billion of benzene. Benzene is something that causes cancer in people.
The European Union and Canada ban potassium bromate, an ingredient added to some flour. The US does not.
Consumer Reports tested 28 dark chocolate bars. All 28 had lead, cadmium, or both.
California researchers looked at 25 tests on kids. In 12 of them, no link was found between food colors and behavior.

Questions

Do I have to buy all 7?

No. Start with Step 1. You can add the other parts later, one at a time, or buy all 7 together.

Do the 7 PDFs work together?

Yes. They use the same 10-second check and the same 12 names, and they point to each other. The 12-Week Plan tells you which pages to use each week.

Do I have to print them?

No. You can type in the PDFs on a computer, tablet, or phone. You can also print any page and write on it by hand.

Will it work on my phone?

Yes, in the free Adobe Acrobat Reader app. Typing is easiest on a computer or a tablet. Many basic PDF apps cannot type in the boxes, so use Adobe Acrobat Reader.

Does it tell me which brands are bad?

No. Companies change recipes, and the same product can list different ingredients in different places. The system shows you how to check the label in your own hand and keep your own lists.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is for information only. It does not say any food will cure or cause an illness in you. If you have a health condition, or you are changing what a child eats, ask your doctor.

Where do the facts come from?

Each page says where a fact comes from, such as a named test or report. Some sources are stronger than others, and the page says so.
